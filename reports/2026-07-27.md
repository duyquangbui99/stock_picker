market cap. Flat revenue plus a ~20% reduction in shares is double-digit growth
in free cash flow per share, which is the only figure that matters to a holder.
At an enterprise value of roughly $607M ($551M cap plus ~$56M net debt), you are
paying single-digit multiples of cash flow for 91% of ARR concentrated in
customers spending $50K+ — the sticky end of the book, which grew 1% while the
sub-$50K long tail fell 19%. The erosion is in the segment that was never going
to be durable anyway.

- Revenue trend: Q1 FY27 revenue $107.9M, -1% YoY vs $109.5M. ARR $440.8M,
  roughly flat. ARR from customers ≥$50K ACV: $403.1M, +1% YoY (91% of total).
  ARR below $50K: $37.7M, -19% YoY. Net retention 97%. This is a slow decline,

MAIN RISK:
AI-driven search disintermediates the business Yext is built on. Yext monetizes
managing a brand's structured presence across search engines, maps and
directories; if answer engines route users without ever touching those listings,
the ≥$50K enterprise cohort stops being sticky and the 1% decline becomes a 10%
decline. A no-growth asset at 8x cash flow is cheap; a melting one is not.
Compounding this, management has suspended both forward guidance and quarterly
earnings calls — so the shareholder's ability to see that inflection coming has
been deliberately reduced.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND:
If ARR from customers with $50K+ annual contract value — the $403.1M cohort —
turns negative year over year in any quarter, or net retention drops below 95%,
the thesis is broken and the buyback is just financial engineering on a shrinking
base. Conversely, if the company restores earnings calls and ARR holds flat for
two more quarters while share count keeps falling, this rerates.
