Those are genuinely good bank returns, not screen artifacts, and they are being produced organically through net interest margin expansion (3.64% in Q1 2026, guided to 3.55–3.65% for the year) rather than through acquisition accounting — which is precisely what disqualified Mid Penn Bancorp, whose 39.6% "revenue growth" is almost entirely the William Penn and 1st Colonial deals. Management is signalling confidence with capital: the quarterly dividend was raised 16.7% to $0.14, and insiders have been net buyers of roughly $491k of stock over the last twelve months. At a market cap of $770M (21 Jul 2026) and 1.08x book versus a 1.26x regional-bank peer average, you are paying a below-peer multiple for above-peer returns on equity. I want to be honest about the limit of this thesis: at $23.72 the stock just set a 52-week high and Piper Sandler's raised target is $24.00, so this is not a mispricing trade — it is a durable, fairly-priced compounder for a portfolio where survival matters more than upside, which is exactly the mandate.
